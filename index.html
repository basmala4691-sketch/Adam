Discover the life of Adam Smith, the philosopher whose ideas helped shape the foundations of modern economic thought.

Adam Smith was born in Kirkcaldy, Fife, Scotland, in 1723. His exact birth date is unknown, although he was baptised on 5 June 1723. He became one of the most influential figures of the Scottish Enlightenment, and his education exposed him to philosophy, moral reasoning, science and the debates of his age.

The world that shaped his thinking

The Scottish Enlightenment was a flowering of debate in Edinburgh and Glasgow about philosophy, morality, commerce, trade, government and human behaviour. Smith lived among its thinkers and learned to ask how people judge right and wrong, and how societies prosper.

He also lived as Britain’s commerce, trade and manufacturing were expanding, the early stages of what would later be called the Industrial Revolution. That setting informed his observations, but his work did not single-handedly cause it.

Beyond the books

Smith also wrote essays and delivered lectures on philosophy, jurisprudence, rhetoric and other subjects. Not every lecture survives as a complete published book; some are known through students’ notes.
